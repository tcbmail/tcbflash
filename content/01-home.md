# Ben Hornung

I’m Ben Hornung. I grew up in Ames, Iowa, and as a kid I wanted to be wherever they kept the good computers. Around the time I was eighteen, that was a place called Rave Studios. I got to use their video equipment to cut old family movies to music. You could move a cut by a second and change how the same little bit of film felt. I could happily spend an afternoon on that.

These days I live in Missouri and I’m building a company called FlashForce. We’re developing AI employees for small businesses. Working on it brought back something I hadn’t felt in years: I’ll sit down at a computer to try one idea, get it half working, and discover there’s nothing else I’d rather do tonight than figure out the other half.

## The business I have in mind

I spent years selling used car parts online. I’m perfectly happy to pull a part, take its picture and pack it when it sells. What I’d love to hand somebody is the job in between. Figure out what it fits. Decide what we ought to charge. Write a listing I’m willing to stand behind. Come get me when the number on the part is too worn to read.

I know how to explain that job to somebody. I don’t want to have to explain it again every time I take another picture. That’s the distinction I’m working on at FlashForce. Before I ask another business owner to trust an AI with an ongoing job, I want to know what it takes to trust one with mine. [Here’s the first job we tried.](#carmen)

## What I’m thinking about

I’ve been on both sides of a fairly aggravating experience: having an idea I couldn’t afford to build, and getting far enough to build it and discovering what was wrong with the idea. I much prefer the second one. [Talentless Doesn’t Mean Visionless](#vision) is about why I’d like more people to get that chance. It started with a piece of music I couldn’t have played in a million years.

Then there’s a part of this business I’m less comfortable with. I want a small shop to be able to afford the kind of help a big company takes for granted. I also want to know what the people who used to earn their living doing that work are supposed to live on. Building FlashForce has changed my mind about what we owe them. I wrote about that in [Who Gets the Hour?](#hour)

## Away from work

My idea of a good afternoon is fishing from the bank below a dam. Nickajack in Tennessee is a favorite. I like a light rod, a little jig and the possibility that the next fish will be something completely different from the last one. I also have more vintage Tommy Bahama shirts than the occasion strictly requires.
